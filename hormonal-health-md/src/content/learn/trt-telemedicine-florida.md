Yes, a large part of TRT care can be delivered by telemedicine in Florida. As of November 2026, federal rules still permit a clinician to prescribe a controlled medication such as testosterone after a video visit, without an in-person exam first. That permission is temporary, and it is scheduled to end on December 31, 2026 unless it is extended or replaced.

What the law allows and what a careful clinic chooses to do are two separate things. This clinic's own policy: [[CONFIRM: telehealth prescribing policy]]

## What does Florida law say?

Florida allows it. Testosterone is a Schedule III controlled substance, and [Florida's telehealth statute](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0400-0499/0456/Sections/0456.47.html) was amended in 2022 to let telehealth providers prescribe Schedule III, IV and V medications. The tighter restrictions that remain apply to Schedule II drugs.

The clinician has to be licensed in Florida or registered with the state as an out-of-state telehealth provider, and you have to be physically in Florida at the time of the visit.

## What does federal law say?

Federal law is stricter, and it is the part that keeps moving. The Ryan Haight Act of 2008 generally requires at least one in-person medical evaluation before a controlled substance is prescribed. In March 2020 that requirement was relaxed because of the pandemic.

The relaxation has been extended four times. The [latest extension](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2025-24123), issued by the DEA and the Department of Health and Human Services, took effect on January 1, 2026 and runs through December 31, 2026. The DEA has proposed permanent rules, including a special registration for telemedicine prescribers, but as of November 2026 the temporary rule is what is in force.

If you are reading this after that date, check the current position before you rely on it.

## So do I need an in-person visit?

Legally, not at the moment. Practically, it depends on the clinic and on you. Many clinicians want to examine a patient at least once before starting a controlled medication for long-term use, because blood pressure, body measurements and a physical exam add information a video call cannot.

## Which parts of TRT work well by video?

Most of the talking does. These visits rarely need you in the room:

- The first conversation about symptoms and history
- Reviewing your lab results
- Deciding on a form and dose
- Routine follow-ups once you are stable
- Questions about side effects

## What always has to be done in person?

Anything that involves your body directly:

- **Blood draws.** Two morning tests before diagnosis, then monitoring labs at three to six months and at least yearly, as the [Endocrine Society](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229) recommends.
- **A physical exam**, when your clinician needs one.
- **Blood pressure and measurements.**
- **Pellet placement** or a clinic-administered injection, if that is the form you use.

## What are the warning signs of a poor telehealth service?

Convenience is fine. Skipped steps are not. Be cautious of any service that:

- Offers testosterone without two morning blood tests
- Does not ask about fertility plans, prostate health or sleep apnea
- Has no schedule for follow-up labs
- Never lets you speak with the clinician who is prescribing
- Treats a single borderline result as a diagnosis

Guidelines require symptoms and confirmed low levels before treatment. A service that shortcuts that is selling testosterone, not treating a condition.

## What happens if the rules change while I am on treatment?

If the federal allowance ends without a replacement, patients who started by video may need an in-person evaluation to keep receiving prescriptions. Having a physical clinic you can visit removes that uncertainty. It is one practical reason to choose a provider with a real location in Florida.
