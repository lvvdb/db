## What is hormone therapy for women?

Hormone therapy replaces the estrogen your ovaries make less of during perimenopause and after menopause. If you have a uterus, it is paired with a progestogen to protect the uterine lining. It is prescribed to relieve symptoms such as hot flashes, night sweats, broken sleep and vaginal dryness, and it also helps prevent bone loss.

It is not one product. Dose, form and timing are chosen for you, then reviewed as your body and your symptoms change.

## What does bioidentical actually mean?

Bioidentical means the hormone is chemically identical to the one your body produces. That describes many standard, FDA-approved prescriptions, including estradiol in patches, gels and tablets and progesterone in capsules. You do not need a custom-mixed product to get a bioidentical hormone.

The word is also used to sell compounded hormones, which a pharmacy mixes to order. Those are not reviewed by the FDA, so the dose in each batch can vary. [The Menopause Society](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002028) and a [National Academies review](https://doi.org/10.17226/25791) both advise using approved products unless there is a specific medical reason, such as an allergy to an ingredient.

## Who is hormone therapy right for?

For healthy women with bothersome symptoms who are under 60 or within 10 years of their last period, the benefits of hormone therapy generally outweigh the risks. That is the position of The Menopause Society and the Endocrine Society. Starting later, or with certain health conditions, changes the balance and needs a closer look.

Hormone therapy is usually not recommended if you have:

- A history of breast cancer or another estrogen-sensitive cancer
- Unexplained vaginal bleeding that has not been evaluated
- A previous blood clot, stroke or heart attack
- Active liver disease

If hormones are not right for you, there are effective non-hormonal prescriptions for hot flashes, and we will go through those instead.

## What symptoms does it help?

Hormone therapy is the most effective treatment available for hot flashes and night sweats. It also treats vaginal dryness, pain with sex and urinary symptoms caused by low estrogen, and it prevents bone loss. Sleep, mood and concentration often improve as night sweats settle.

It is not a treatment for general tiredness or weight on its own. If your symptoms are less clear, start with [menopause symptoms](/conditions/menopause), [perimenopause](/conditions/perimenopause) or [always tired](/conditions/always-tired) to see what else is worth checking.

## Do I need blood tests first?

Most women over 45 do not need hormone levels measured to diagnose menopause or to start treatment. The pattern of your periods and your symptoms tells the story. What matters more is a health check: blood pressure, your personal and family history, and screening that is up to date, including a mammogram.

Labs are useful to rule out conditions that mimic menopause. Thyroid tests, a blood count, iron stores and blood sugar are common ones. Under 45, or when the picture is unclear, hormone testing has a bigger role. Our [lab testing page](/lab-testing) explains the panels. [[CONFIRM: panels and prices]]

## What are the options: patches, gels, pills, vaginal estrogen or pellets?

The right form depends on your symptoms and your risk profile. Estrogen through the skin avoids the liver and, in observational studies, carries a lower risk of blood clots than tablets. Vaginal estrogen treats local symptoms with very little reaching the rest of the body.

| Form | Used for | Worth knowing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Estradiol patch, gel or spray | Hot flashes, sleep, bone protection | Steady dosing through the skin. Often the first choice. |
| Estradiol tablets | Hot flashes, sleep, bone protection | Simple and low cost. Slightly higher clot risk than skin forms. |
| Vaginal estrogen cream, tablet or ring | Dryness, pain with sex, urinary symptoms | Low dose, local effect. Can be used alone or alongside other forms. |
| Micronized progesterone | Protecting the uterine lining | Taken at bedtime. Often helps sleep. |
| Pellets | Marketed for long-acting dosing | Compounded and not FDA-approved. The dose cannot be adjusted once placed. |

## Why do I need progesterone?

If you have a uterus, estrogen on its own thickens the uterine lining and raises the risk of endometrial cancer. A progestogen prevents that. Micronized progesterone is the bioidentical option and is usually taken as a capsule at night. If you have had a hysterectomy, you generally do not need it.

## What are the risks?

Risks depend on your age, how long since menopause, the type of hormone, the dose and the route. For most healthy women starting before 60, serious problems are uncommon. The main ones to understand are blood clots and stroke, which are more linked to tablets than to patches or gels, and a small increase in breast cancer risk with longer use of combined estrogen and progestogen.

We go through your personal risk before you start, use the lowest dose that controls your symptoms and review the plan at least once a year. There is no fixed date when you must stop. The decision to continue is made with you, based on how you feel and how your health has changed.

## What about testosterone for women?

Testosterone has one evidence-based use in women: low sexual desire that causes distress after menopause, once other causes have been addressed. That is the conclusion of a [global consensus statement](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2019-01603) from menopause and endocrine societies. It is not recommended for energy, mood, bone health or wellbeing in general.

There is no FDA-approved testosterone product for women in the United States, so any use needs careful dosing and blood monitoring to keep levels in the normal female range. [[CONFIRM: whether testosterone is prescribed for women at this clinic, and in what form]]

## How do I get started?

Take the [two-minute assessment](/assessment) or call the clinic. Your first visit covers your symptoms, your cycle history, your family history and what you want from treatment. If labs or screening are needed, we arrange them before anything is prescribed.
