## What is adult growth hormone deficiency?

Adult growth hormone deficiency is a condition where the pituitary gland does not make enough growth hormone. It is rare. In adults it almost always follows something specific: a pituitary tumor, surgery or radiation near the pituitary, a serious head injury, or a deficiency that began in childhood.

It is not the same as the gradual fall in growth hormone that happens to everyone with age. That decline is normal and is not a disease.

## What are the symptoms?

The symptoms are real but not specific. Adults with a true deficiency often report low energy, more fat around the middle, less muscle and stamina, low mood and poorer quality of life. Blood tests may show unfavorable cholesterol and scans may show lower bone density.

Every one of those has more common causes. That is why symptoms alone never make the diagnosis, and why we look for the likelier explanations first: [low testosterone](/conditions/low-testosterone), thyroid problems, sleep, weight and mood.

## How is growth hormone deficiency diagnosed?

Diagnosis needs proof that the pituitary cannot release growth hormone when stimulated. Growth hormone is released in pulses, so a random blood level tells you nothing. A blood test called IGF-1 is a reasonable screen, but it can be normal in people who are deficient and low in people who are not.

For most adults, the [Endocrine Society guideline](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2011-0179) calls for a stimulation test. You are given a medication that should trigger growth hormone release, and blood is drawn several times over a few hours to see whether it does. These tests are run under medical supervision, usually by an endocrinologist.

The main exception is someone with clear pituitary disease and deficiencies in three or more other pituitary hormones along with a low IGF-1. In that situation the diagnosis can be made without a stimulation test.

## Who can be prescribed growth hormone?

Growth hormone is approved for adults with a confirmed deficiency and for a small number of other specific conditions, such as muscle wasting from HIV and short bowel syndrome. Outside those uses, prescribing it is not a gray area.

Unlike most medications, human growth hormone cannot legally be prescribed off-label. [Federal law](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/333) makes it an offense to distribute it for any use the FDA has not authorized. Anti-aging, weight loss, athletic performance and bodybuilding are not authorized uses.

## Do you prescribe growth hormone to healthy adults?

No. We do not prescribe growth hormone to healthy adults for aging, energy, physique or performance. It is against federal law, and the evidence does not support it. A [systematic review](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-146-2-200701160-00005) of trials in healthy older adults found small changes in body composition and frequent side effects, including swelling, joint pain and carpal tunnel syndrome.

If someone offers you growth hormone without stimulation testing and a documented diagnosis, that is a warning sign about the clinic, not a shortcut.

## What are the risks of growth hormone treatment?

For people with a confirmed deficiency, treatment is started at a low dose and adjusted by IGF-1 level and symptoms. Side effects are mostly dose related: fluid retention, joint and muscle aches, carpal tunnel symptoms and higher blood sugar. It is not used in people with active cancer.

Treatment needs regular monitoring by a clinician experienced with it, which is one more reason the diagnosis has to be solid before anyone starts.

## If it is not growth hormone, what else could it be?

Usually something more common and easier to treat. For most adults who feel tired, soft and flat, testing turns up one of these instead:

- [Low testosterone](/conditions/low-testosterone) in men
- [Perimenopause](/conditions/perimenopause) or [menopause](/conditions/menopause) in women
- An underactive thyroid, low iron or low vitamin B12
- Sleep apnea or simply too little sleep
- Blood sugar problems and weight gain, which [medical weight loss](/treatments/medical-weight-loss) addresses
- Depression, stress or medication side effects
