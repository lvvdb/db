## What causes joint pain?

The most common cause in adults is osteoarthritis, the gradual wearing of cartilage in knees, hips, hands and the spine. Other causes include inflammatory arthritis such as rheumatoid arthritis, gout, tendon and ligament problems, and old injuries. Each behaves differently and is treated differently.

Osteoarthritis tends to hurt more with use and ease with rest. Inflammatory arthritis is often worse in the morning, with stiffness lasting more than half an hour and visible swelling. Telling them apart is the first job.

## How do you evaluate joint pain?

We ask where it hurts, when, and what makes it better or worse, then examine the joints. Blood work is chosen from that picture. It may include markers of inflammation, uric acid for gout, antibody tests for rheumatoid arthritis and vitamin D. If imaging or a specialist opinion is needed, we refer you.

## What does the evidence support?

For osteoarthritis of the knee, hip and hand, the [American College of Rheumatology guideline](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.41142) strongly recommends exercise, weight loss for people who carry extra weight, and self-management programs. Among medications, it supports anti-inflammatory gels for the knee, anti-inflammatory tablets when they are safe for you, and steroid injections for flares.

The same guideline recommends against several heavily marketed options for knee and hip osteoarthritis, including PRP injections, stem cell injections and glucosamine. We would rather you spend your money on what works.

## Does weight affect my joints?

Yes. Extra body weight loads the knees and hips with every step, and fat tissue also drives inflammation. For people with knee or hip osteoarthritis who are overweight, losing weight reduces pain and improves function.
