## What counts as erectile dysfunction?

Erectile dysfunction means regularly being unable to get or keep an erection firm enough for sex. An occasional off night is normal. When it happens most of the time for a few months, or it is getting steadily worse, it is worth finding out why.

It is one of the most common reasons men see a doctor, and one of the most treatable.

## What causes ED?

Most ED comes from reduced blood flow, nerve signals that are not getting through, hormones, medication, or the mind getting in the way. In practice several overlap. The usual suspects are:

- **Blood vessel disease**, from high blood pressure, high cholesterol or smoking
- **Diabetes**, which damages both vessels and nerves
- **Low testosterone**, which lowers desire first
- **Medication side effects**
- **Excess weight, heavy drinking and poor sleep**, including sleep apnea
- **Stress, anxiety, depression and relationship strain**
- **Pelvic surgery or injury**, such as prostate surgery

## What does the pattern tell me?

Quite a lot. Gradual onset, the same problem with a partner and alone, and fewer morning erections suggest a physical cause. A sudden start, good erections on waking or alone, and trouble only in certain situations suggest stress or performance anxiety is the bigger factor.

Neither pattern is a diagnosis. It simply tells us where to look first.

## Why does ED matter for my heart?

The arteries that supply the penis are narrower than those that supply the heart, so they can show the effects of narrowing earlier. For that reason the [American Urological Association](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.05.004) treats ED as a risk marker for cardiovascular disease, and advises that men with ED be told so.

That makes an ED visit a good moment to check blood pressure, cholesterol and blood sugar, even if you feel fine otherwise.

## What should I test?

Start with a morning testosterone level, a blood sugar test, a cholesterol panel and a blood pressure reading. Together they cover the hormonal, metabolic and blood vessel causes behind most erectile dysfunction, and guidelines recommend the testosterone test for every man with ED.

- **Morning total testosterone**, recommended for every man with ED
- **Fasting glucose or A1c**
- **Cholesterol panel**
- **Blood pressure**
- **Prolactin or thyroid tests**, when your history suggests them

## When should I see someone urgently?

Go to an emergency department for an erection that lasts more than four hours, or for chest pain during sex or exercise. Book a prompt visit for ED that starts right after an injury to the pelvis or groin, a new painful curve in the penis, or ED alongside leg weakness or numbness.

## What does treatment look like?

Treatment follows the cause. For many men that means improving blood vessel health, adjusting a medication and using an ED tablet. If testosterone is low, replacing it can restore desire and improve the response to tablets. Other options exist when tablets are not enough.
