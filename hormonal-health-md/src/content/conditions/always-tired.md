## Why am I always tired?

Persistent tiredness usually comes from one of a short list of causes: not enough good sleep, low iron, a thyroid problem, blood sugar problems, low mood, medication, or a hormone change. Often two or three stack up. The useful question is which ones apply to you, and that is something a history and blood work can answer.

Fatigue that lasts more than a few weeks and is not explained by an obvious life event deserves a proper look.

## What are the most common medical causes?

The usual causes are too little or poor-quality sleep, sleep apnea, low iron, an underactive thyroid, blood sugar problems, low vitamin B12, depression or anxiety, and medication side effects. Each one leaves clues, and each can be checked.

| Cause | Clues | How it is checked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poor or short sleep | Under seven hours, irregular schedule, late screens or alcohol | Sleep history |
| Sleep apnea | Snoring, waking unrefreshed, morning headaches | Screening questions, then a sleep study |
| Low iron or anemia | Heavy periods, breathlessness, pale skin, restless legs | Blood count and ferritin |
| Underactive thyroid | Feeling cold, weight gain, dry skin, constipation | TSH blood test |
| Diabetes or prediabetes | Thirst, frequent urination, energy crashes | A1c or fasting glucose |
| Low vitamin B12 | Numbness or tingling, a restricted diet, long-term metformin or acid reducers | B12 level |
| Depression or anxiety | Low mood, loss of interest, poor sleep | Conversation and screening |
| Medication | Started after a new prescription or dose change | Medication review |

## Could it be my hormones?

It could. In men, low testosterone causes fatigue along with lower sex drive, fewer morning erections and loss of muscle. In women in their 40s and 50s, [perimenopause](/conditions/perimenopause) and [menopause](/conditions/menopause) disrupt sleep and energy, often before periods change much.

Hormones are rarely the whole story, which is why we test them together with the basics. See [signs of low testosterone](/conditions/low-testosterone) if that list sounds familiar.

## What should I test?

A sensible first round is a blood count, ferritin, thyroid function, blood sugar, kidney and liver function, and B12. We add a morning testosterone for men with matching symptoms, vitamin D when you have risk factors for being low, and a pregnancy test where relevant.

If your results come back normal, that is useful too. It shifts attention to sleep, mood, workload and medication, which blood tests cannot measure. Our [lab testing page](/lab-testing) lists the panels. [[CONFIRM: panels and prices]]

## When is fatigue a warning sign?

Tiredness needs prompt medical attention when it arrives with unexplained weight loss, fever, night sweats, breathlessness, chest pain, bleeding or fainting. Those combinations can point to something serious, so do not wait for a routine appointment. See a doctor soon if you have any of these:

- Weight loss you cannot explain
- Fevers or drenching night sweats
- Shortness of breath or chest pain
- Black or bloody stools
- Fainting, or a racing or irregular heartbeat
- New severe headaches or weakness on one side

Thoughts of harming yourself need help right away. Call or text 988 at any hour.

## What does treatment look like?

Treatment matches the cause. Iron and B12 are replaced when low. Thyroid problems are treated with thyroid hormone. Sleep apnea is treated with a device worn at night. Hormone problems are treated on their own terms.
