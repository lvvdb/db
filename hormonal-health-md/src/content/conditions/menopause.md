## What are the symptoms of menopause?

Menopause is the point 12 months after your final period, and for most women it arrives in the early 50s. The hallmark symptoms are hot flashes and night sweats. Sleep problems, vaginal dryness, pain with sex, urinary urgency, mood changes and difficulty concentrating are also common.

Not everyone gets every symptom, and severity varies widely. If your periods are still coming but have become unpredictable, you are more likely in [perimenopause](/conditions/perimenopause), which has its own pattern.

## Why does it happen?

Your ovaries gradually stop releasing eggs and make much less estrogen and progesterone. Estrogen acts on the brain's temperature control, on sleep, on bone, and on the tissues of the vagina and bladder, so falling levels show up in all of those places.

Menopause can also happen suddenly after surgery to remove the ovaries, or after some cancer treatments. Symptoms are often more intense when it happens that way.

## What should I test?

Often nothing hormonal. If you are over 45 and your periods have stopped for a year, the diagnosis is made from your history. What is worth checking is your overall health, because the years after menopause are when heart, bone and metabolic risks start to rise.

A sensible check includes blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, thyroid function and a review of whether your mammogram and other screening are up to date. Under 45, or if the picture is unclear, we add hormone tests. See [lab testing](/lab-testing). [[CONFIRM: panels and prices]]

## What else could it be?

Thyroid problems, low iron, depression, anxiety, medication side effects and sleep apnea can all imitate menopause. They cause sweats, palpitations, exhaustion and mood changes of their own, and they are common at the same age, so they are worth ruling out:

- **Thyroid problems**, which cause heat intolerance, sweats, palpitations and mood changes
- **Low iron or anemia**, which cause fatigue and poor concentration
- **Depression and anxiety**, which often flare at this stage of life anyway
- **Medication side effects**, including some antidepressants
- **Sleep apnea**, which becomes more common after menopause

If exhaustion is your main complaint, read [always tired](/conditions/always-tired).

## When should I see someone sooner?

Any vaginal bleeding after menopause needs to be evaluated, even if it is light or happens once. See a clinician promptly as well if your periods stop before 40, or if hot flashes come with unexplained weight loss, fevers or a racing heart.

## What does treatment look like?

For healthy women under 60 or within 10 years of their last period, hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for hot flashes and night sweats, according to [The Menopause Society](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002028). Low-dose vaginal estrogen treats dryness and urinary symptoms. Non-hormonal prescriptions are available when hormones are not suitable.
