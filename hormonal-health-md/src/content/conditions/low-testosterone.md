## What are the signs of low testosterone?

The symptoms most specific to low testosterone are sexual: less desire, fewer morning erections and erections that are less reliable. Other signs include loss of body hair, hot flushes, and reduced muscle and bone strength. Tiredness, low mood, poor focus and extra belly fat are common too, but they have many other causes.

If you tick several boxes in the list above, especially the sexual ones, a blood test is worth doing. If tiredness is your only symptom, low testosterone is possible but not the likeliest answer. Start with [always tired](/conditions/always-tired).

## What causes testosterone to run low?

There are two broad groups of causes. In the first, the testicles themselves are not producing enough, because of a genetic condition, an injury, an infection or past chemotherapy. In the second, the brain is not sending the signal to make it.

The second group is far more common in adult men, and much of it is treatable:

- Excess weight and type 2 diabetes
- Untreated sleep apnea and chronic poor sleep
- Opioid pain medication and long-term steroid tablets
- Past or current anabolic steroid use
- Heavy alcohol use
- A pituitary problem, which is uncommon but important to find

Treating the cause can bring levels back up without testosterone itself.

## What should I test?

Start with a total testosterone level drawn in the morning, ideally fasting. Testosterone peaks early in the day, so an afternoon sample can read falsely low. If the first result is low, the [Endocrine Society](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229) recommends repeating it on a different morning before calling it a diagnosis.

A proper workup then adds LH and FSH to locate the cause, and often free testosterone, prolactin, a blood count and a PSA depending on your age and results. Do not test while you are sick or right after a hard run of poor sleep, because short-term illness lowers testosterone temporarily. Panels are on our [lab testing page](/lab-testing). ## What else could it be?

Thyroid problems, depression, sleep apnea, diabetes, medication side effects and blood vessel disease all cause the same tiredness, low mood and low sex drive as low testosterone. Many men have more than one at once, which is why they are tested together:

- **Thyroid problems**, which cause fatigue, weight change and low mood
- **Depression or chronic stress**
- **Sleep apnea**, especially if you snore or wake unrefreshed
- **Diabetes or prediabetes**
- **Medication side effects**, including some antidepressants and blood pressure tablets
- **Blood vessel disease**, when [erection problems](/conditions/erectile-dysfunction) are the main issue

A good evaluation tests for these alongside testosterone, so you are not treated for the wrong thing.

## When should I see someone sooner?

Book promptly if low desire or erection problems come with headaches and changes in your vision, milky discharge from the nipples, or breast swelling. Those can point to a pituitary problem. If you and your partner are trying to conceive, say so before any treatment, because testosterone therapy lowers sperm production.

## What does treatment look like?

If two morning tests confirm a low level and you have symptoms, testosterone replacement is one option, alongside treating whatever is driving the low level. It comes as injections, gels and other forms, and it needs monitoring with regular blood work.
