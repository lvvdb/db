## What are the signs of perimenopause?

Perimenopause is the transition leading up to menopause, and the first sign is usually a change in your cycle. Periods come closer together or further apart, and flow can become heavier or lighter. Sleep problems, mood changes, hot flashes, night sweats and brain fog often follow, sometimes while periods still look fairly regular.

Many women are told they are too young or that their labs are normal. Neither rules it out.

## Why does it happen?

In the years before menopause, the ovaries respond less predictably. Estrogen does not simply decline. It swings, sometimes higher than before and sometimes very low, while progesterone falls as ovulation becomes less regular. Those swings are why symptoms can appear suddenly and vary from month to month.

Doctors stage the transition by cycle pattern. Cycles that differ in length by a week or more mark the early stage. Gaps of 60 days or more mark the late stage.

## What should I test?

There is no single test that confirms perimenopause. If you are over 45, guidelines support making the diagnosis from your symptoms and cycle changes. FSH and estradiol move too much during this stage to settle the question with one sample.

Testing is still useful, because it rules out the look-alikes:

- **Thyroid function**
- **Blood count and ferritin**, especially with heavy periods
- **Blood sugar or A1c**
- **A pregnancy test**, when a period is late
- **Hormone levels**, if you are under 45 or the picture is unclear

## What else could it be?

Thyroid disease, iron deficiency, depression, anxiety and chronic sleep loss all overlap with perimenopause, and they often occur together with it. Heavy periods can cause iron deficiency, which then causes exhaustion in its own right. If tiredness dominates, see [always tired](/conditions/always-tired).

## When should I see someone sooner?

Changing periods are expected, but some bleeding patterns need a prompt evaluation:

- Bleeding heavy enough to soak a pad or tampon every hour for several hours
- Bleeding that lasts much longer than usual
- Bleeding between periods or after sex
- Periods that come more often than every three weeks

Low mood that affects daily life also deserves attention now. The risk of depression rises during this transition, and it is treatable.

## What does treatment look like?

Treatment depends on your main symptoms and whether you need contraception. Options include hormone therapy, hormonal contraception that steadies cycles and symptoms together, a hormonal IUD for heavy bleeding, and non-hormonal medication for hot flashes, mood or sleep. Iron is replaced if it is low.
